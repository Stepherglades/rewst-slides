# Rewst Brand Reference

The canonical source is the Rewst public style guide. This file condenses the
parts that govern a slide deck. When the style guide and anything else disagree,
the style guide wins.

## The non-negotiables (read first)

1. **Bot Teal is the anchor.** Primary Bot Teal is **`#00BBB4`** — the one value
   the team standardized on. Every slide must carry at least one element in this
   exact shade (see "The Bot Teal rule" below). The marketing site once drifted
   to `#1EAFAF`; that value is retired here — do not reintroduce it.
2. **Solid fills only. No gradients between brand hues.** The look is flat and
   confident, not glossy.
3. **Stay inside the palette.** Don't invent hues. Neutrals are connective
   tissue, never the headline.
4. **Poppins for headings, Montserrat for body.** Nothing else.
5. **Use the official logo files, give them room, don't get clever.**

## Color

Four families, each a saturated core flanked by darker and lighter cousins.
Pair a saturated color with its own softer tint for depth; don't pair two
saturated hues against each other.

### Bot Teal — primary brand family (anchor every layout here)
| Name | Hex | Typical use |
|------|-----|-------------|
| Bot Teal Deep | `#082C2C` | Slide backgrounds, footers, cards |
| Bot Teal Dark | `#005655` | Section backgrounds, alt surfaces |
| **Bot Teal (primary)** | **`#00BBB4`** | **Accents, eyebrows, glyphs, indicators — the rule color** |
| Bot Teal Light | `#78CFCF` | Light accent |
| Bot Teal Pale | `#A5DFDF` | Pill/card backgrounds, headlines on dark |

### Ops Indigo — secondary
`#141121` · `#282242` · **`#504384`** (primary) · `#968EB5` · `#BAB3CF`

### Trigger Amber — warm / CTA (use sparingly; it means "do this")
`#3F2900` · `#7D5100` · **`#F9A100`** (primary CTA) · `#FBC766` · `#FDD999`

### Alert Coral — small accents / alerts (never headers)
`#3C1717` · `#792E2E` · **`#F15B5B`** (primary) · `#F79D9D` · `#F9BDBD`

### Neutrals
White `#FFFFFF` · `#E6E6E6` · `#D9D9D9` · Black `#000000`

**Color do / don't**
- Do anchor every layout in Bot Teal — dark or primary.
- Do pair a saturated color with its softer tint for depth.
- Do use Trigger Amber sparingly — it's the CTA color, not decoration.
- Do use Alert Coral only as a small accent — a glyph, a tag, or a single box
  in a set — never a large fill or a layout's anchor. Coral is the canonical
  `#F15B5B`. Sanctioned limited uses: the process step badges, "cons / before"
  check-X glyphs, a single small status tag (`.tag-coral`), a stat delta or
  inline highlight (`.accent-coral`), or one box in a multi-box set. That's the
  whole list — if a use isn't one of these, it's probably too much coral.
- Do reach for coral in a multi-box layout only when Trigger Amber is already
  used on one of the boxes; coral joins as a second accent, not on its own.
- Don't use coral for headers or headlines — those are reserved for the Teal
  shades and Trigger Amber.
- Don't use gradients between two brand hues.
- Don't introduce hues outside this palette.
- Don't lead with neutrals — they support, they don't headline.

## Typography

- **Headings — Poppins** (bold/700). *Goldplay* is the proprietary print display
  face; Poppins is its on-screen stand-in and what every deck uses.
- **Body — Montserrat**, default weight **500** (Rewst body weight), 1.5 line
  height. Poppins is installed in the render environment; Montserrat usually is
  not — drop the Montserrat `.ttf` files into `assets/fonts/` for pixel-perfect
  PDF/PPTX body text (headings are unaffected either way).
- **Eyebrows** — Montserrat bold, uppercase, wide letter-spacing, in Bot Teal.
  They're the most common place primary teal shows up on a slide.
- Headlines run tight on their leading; body stays comfortable.
- **Balance the lines; no widows.** Wrapped headings, titles, and body should
  break into visually balanced lines — never a lone word stranded on its own
  line, and never a single word on the last line. The stylesheet does this
  automatically (`text-wrap: balance` on headings, `pretty` on body), so most
  copy is handled for you. If a stubborn case slips through, bind the last two
  words with a non-breaking space in the HTML (e.g. `do&nbsp;that?`) or shorten
  the copy. (This applies to the rendered formats — HTML, PDF, faithful PPTX;
  the editable PPTX lets PowerPoint re-wrap, so re-check there after editing.)

## Logo & mascot

- **Stewart** (the mascot) and the **Rewst wordmark** are the most-recognized
  pieces of the brand. They earn that by appearing the same way every time.
- Use the official files in `assets/.../logo/`. On dark backgrounds use
  `rewst-logo-ondark.png`; on light/cream use `rewst-logo.png`.
- Give the logo clear space. Never recolor, stretch, rotate, add effects to, or
  reconstruct it. The off-the-edge bleed Stewart on title/divider slides is
  generated by the stylesheet (a CSS silhouette mask filled with the current
  tone-on-tone color) — it is **not** a colored PNG. The only Stewart silhouette
  image asset is `stewart-outline-white.png` (pure white). There is intentionally
  **no teal Stewart PNG**: a retired-teal (`#1EAFAF`) outline once lived here and
  was removed, so it can't be swapped in by accident. Never reintroduce a colored
  Stewart image — use the masked bleed for teal/amber silhouettes.

## Voice & tone

The reader has run a help-desk shift: practical, time-poor, money-sensitive,
and tired of marketing-speak.

- **Get to the point.** Lead with what changes for them.
- **Active voice.** "Rewst cuts ticket time," not "ticket time is cut."
- **Specific over vague.** Real numbers, named workflows, concrete outcomes.
- **No trendy jargon.** No "synergy," "leverage," "paradigm."
- **Outcome-led.** Every claim ties back to a result the reader cares about.
- **Approachable, clear, self-aware.** Confident without bragging.

Slide copy: sentence case for titles, written as full sentences. Headlines do
the work on screen; the presenter says the rest. Three points beat four.

## Layout — margins and bleed

- **Keep a right-hand margin on content.** Text, cards, columns, and tables stay
  within the slide's gutter (~110px from the right edge); nothing content-bearing
  runs to the edge. This safe zone also keeps the editable PPTX reliable, since
  PowerPoint wraps text a touch wider than the browser.
- **The single-color bleed Stewart is the one exception — under strict rules.**
  The off-the-edge Stewart silhouette is used **only on title slides and section
  dividers**, never on any other slide (content slides, the close, etc.). It is
  **always off the right edge** (never the left), in the **same placement every
  time**, and **always tone-on-tone**: a teal silhouette on a teal field, or an
  amber silhouette on an amber field — never teal-on-amber or amber-on-teal.
  That bleed is art, not content; it's the only thing allowed to cross the right
  margin.
- **Footers stay put.** The Rewst logo sits in the bottom-left of every slide;
  the slide number sits in the bottom-right of every slide **except the title and
  section dividers**, where the bleed Stewart occupies the lower-right and the
  number is dropped so the two don't compete for space. Footers keep their fixed
  positions, are never swapped or moved, and are not subject to the content
  margin. (Use the on-dark logo on dark/teal fields, the on-light logo on cream.)
  `check` enforces this — including that the number is absent on Stewart slides.
- **Fixed canvas — text must fit.** Slides are a fixed 1920×1080; nothing
  reflows. Keep copy within the length guidance for each slot so it doesn't wrap
  awkwardly or overrun its card/column. If a value is long (a URL, a multi-part
  contact line), give it a full-width row rather than a narrow half-column. When
  in doubt, shorten the copy or pick a roomier archetype. The `check` script
  enforces the right-margin half of this automatically.
- **Boxes need breathing room.** Any content box or card keeps at least 32px
  (`--box-pad-min`) of internal padding so text never touches its edge. Small
  chips, badges, avatars, and table cells are exempt. `check` enforces this.

## The Bot Teal rule (every slide)

At least one element in exactly `#00BBB4` must appear on every slide. The deck
satisfies this two ways, by design:
- Most archetypes carry it naturally — eyebrows, indicators, the title and
  image backgrounds, the featured pricing tier.
- A small Bot Teal **indicator dot** sits in the footer band of *every* slide
  (defined once in `deck.css`), so even archetypes that lead with amber or cream
  comply — and so do any new slides you author. On the all-teal slides the dot
  simply blends into the background that's already Bot Teal.

`scripts/deck.py check` renders the deck and verifies the rule per slide. Treat
a FAIL as a real defect, not a warning.
